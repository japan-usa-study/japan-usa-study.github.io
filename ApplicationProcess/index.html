Application Process

Your complete guide to applying for US graduate programs

1Research and Shortlist Universities

  • Identify universities that match your academic and personal goals.
  • Check application deadlines and specific requirements for each university.
  • Reach out to the faculty member(s) you have identified or to the department's graduate secretary. Building these connections are essential!
  • When emailing faculty, keep the email brief and attach your CV, copies of transcripts, and any test scores.
  • Refer to our University Selection page for more information.

2Prepare Application Documents

Most universities will require the following materials for your applications, but please check each universities' website for details.

  • Transcripts: Collect official transcripts from your previous institutions.
  • Test Scores: Take standardized tests. TOEFL or IELTS is required for all international students, and some programs also require GRE.
  • Statement of Purpose (SOP): Write a compelling essay about your academic goals and why you're applying.
  • Letters of Recommendation: Request references from professors or professionals who know your work.
  • Resume/CV: Highlight your academic, extracurricular, and professional achievements.

From a faculty perspective in STEM, a strong graduate application clearly demonstrates motivation, solid academic preparation, and evidence of research potential. Faculty look for applicants who show a good understanding of their intended research area in the statement, have relevant coursework and technical skills, and demonstrate curiosity and analytical thinking through prior research, projects, or related work experience. Strong letters of recommendation that speak to an applicant's abilities and work ethic are also crucial. Equally important is a well-written and concise statement that explains what the applicant has accomplished, why they want to pursue graduate study, and how their interests align with the potential advisor's research program and the department more broadly.

Score to Aim For

Required TOEFL scores vary from university to university. Please refer to the university's website for their required scores. Learn more about TOEFL score requirements.

  • Top Universities Typically Require Scores >90
  • Mid-Tier Universities Typically Require Scores >80
  • Community Colleges and Some State Schools Typically Require Scores >70

3Submit Applications

  • Create an account on the university's application portal.
  • Pay attention to deadlines and ensure all documents are uploaded correctly.
  • Pay the application fee (if applicable).
  • Don’t hesitate to contact the faculty you have identified or department graduate secretary to let them know you have applied.

4Apply for Scholarships and Financial Aid

  • Research scholarships offered by universities and external organizations.
  • Ask faculty about funding availability. Most departments fund PhD (MSc in some cases) students through research or teaching assistantships.
  • Typically, your financial aid application is a part of the graduate application. You indicate that you are requesting financial aid within the application system.
  • If there are external fellowships, you can apply for those in consultation with the faculty member. Refer to our Financial Support page.

5Await Results

  • Most universities notify applicants via email or their portal. Keep an eye on deadlines for responding to offers.
  • Start preparing for your visa application if you receive an acceptance letter.

6Secure Your Student Visa

  • The university will send you the forms required to obtain a visa. Check the U.S. Embassy Tokyo website for visa application.

7Plan Your Move

  • Arrange housing, whether on-campus or off-campus.
  • Book your flights and prepare essential documents for travel.
  • Attend orientation programs to settle into your new academic life.
